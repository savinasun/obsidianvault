## Evaluation Metrics
---

### BLEU Score (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy)
[Papineni et al., 2002](https://aclanthology.org/P02-1040.pdf). The dominant automatic metric for machine translation and text generation quality.

**Core idea**: measure the **modified n-gram precision** of the hypothesis against one or more reference translations, then penalize for being too short.

**Modified n-gram precision** for order $n$:
$$
p_n = \frac{\sum_{\text{n-gram} \in \hat{y}} \min\!\left(\text{count}(\text{n-gram}, \hat{y}),\, \max_r \text{count}(\text{n-gram}, r)\right)}{\sum_{\text{n-gram} \in \hat{y}} \text{count}(\text{n-gram}, \hat{y})}
$$
The "modified" part clips each n-gram count to its maximum count in any reference (prevents inflating score by repeating common words).

**BLEU score**:
$$
\text{BLEU} = \text{BP} \cdot \exp\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N} w_n \log p_n\right)
$$
where:
- $N = 4$ (1–4 gram precision), $w_n = 1/N$
- **Brevity Penalty**: $\text{BP} = \min\!\left(1, e^{1 - r/c}\right)$, where $c$ = hypothesis length, $r$ = reference length

**Limitations**:
- Measures surface-level overlap, not semantic meaning
- A paraphrase with different words scores poorly despite being correct
- Favors short outputs (hence BP) but doesn't fully compensate
- Poor correlation with human judgment at the sentence level (better at corpus level)

Modern alternatives: **ROUGE** (recall-oriented, used for summarization), **BERTScore** (semantic similarity via BERT embeddings), **METEOR**, **ChrF**.

### Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU)
+ Exclusively in zero-shot and few-shot settings
+ Covers 57 subjects across STEM, the humanities, the social sciences, and more
+ Ranges in difficulty from an elementary level to an advanced professional level, tests both world knowledge and problem solving ability
+ Multiple choices tasks
+ MMLU Strengths:
	+ More challenging than some benchmarks
	+ Identifies areas for LLM improvement
+ [Massive Multitask Language Understanding](https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/mmlu)

### Multi-turn Reasoning Benchmark (MTR-Bench)
[MTR-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Multi-Turn Reasoning Evaluation](https://paperswithcode.com/paper/mtr-bench-a-comprehensive-benchmark-for-multi), [MT-Bench](https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/mt-bench)

+ This dataset contains 3.3K expert-level pairwise human preferences for model responses generated by 6 models in response to 80 MT-bench questions. The 6 models are GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claud-v1, Vicuna-13B, Alpaca-13B, and LLaMA-13B. The annotators are mostly graduate students with expertise in the topic areas of each of the questions.
+ Focus on conversation
+ Evaluate how well an LLM understands and follows instructions provided throughout a conversation
+ Depth of understanding
