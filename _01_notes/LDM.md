# **Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)**

**Tags**:: #generative
**Links**:: [[Autoencoder]] · [[DDPM]] · [[Score-Based Generative Modeling Methods]] · [[VAE]] · [[ELBO]] · [[CLIP]] · [[FID]]


**Latent Diffusion Models** ([Rombach et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)) apply the diffusion process in a **compressed latent space** rather than pixel space. This dramatically reduces computation while preserving image quality — enabling the training of high-resolution generative models at tractable cost. **Stable Diffusion** is the most prominent LDM.

## Motivation: Why Latent Space?
---
Standard [[DDPM]] operates directly on pixel-space images, e.g., $x \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 512 \times 3}$. This has two costs:

1. **Computation**: the U-Net denoiser processes full-resolution tensors at every diffusion step
2. **Redundancy**: most of the variation in an image is low-frequency structure; pixel-level noise is wasteful

LDMs compress the image to a much smaller latent $z \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}$ (typically $64 \times 64 \times 4$ for a $512 \times 512$ image, a $48\times$ reduction in dimensionality), then run diffusion in that compact space.

## Architecture: Two-Stage Design
---

### Stage 1: Perceptual Compression (VAE)
A [[VAE]]-based **encoder–decoder** pair is trained to compress and reconstruct images:

$$
z = \mathcal{E}(x), \quad \tilde{x} = \mathcal{D}(z)
$$

- **Encoder** $\mathcal{E}$: maps image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ → latent $z \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}$
- **Decoder** $\mathcal{D}$: reconstructs $\tilde{x}$ from $z$, trained with a perceptual loss + patch-based discriminator (VQGAN-style)

The encoder/decoder is trained *separately* and kept **frozen** during diffusion training. It only needs to be a good perceptual compressor — it doesn't model the data distribution.

### Stage 2: Diffusion in Latent Space
The [[DDPM]] forward/reverse process is applied to the latent $z$ (not the image):

**Forward**: add noise to latent
$$
q(z_t \mid z_0) = \mathcal{N}(z_t;\, \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, z_0,\, (1-\bar\alpha_t)I)
$$

**Reverse**: a U-Net denoiser $\epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, c)$ predicts the noise added at step $t$, conditioned on context $c$:
$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{LDM} = \mathbb{E}_{z_0, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I), t}\left[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, c)\|^2\right]
$$

At sampling, run the reverse chain from $z_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ to $z_0$, then decode with $\mathcal{D}(z_0)$.

## Conditioning Mechanisms
---
LDMs support flexible conditioning via **cross-attention** layers in the U-Net:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V
$$

where $Q$ comes from the latent features and $K, V$ come from the conditioning signal $c$:

| Conditioning type | $c$ encoding |
|------------------|-------------|
| **Text** (Stable Diffusion) | [[CLIP]] text encoder or T5 |
| **Class label** | Learned class embedding |
| **Image** (image-to-image) | Encoded reference image |
| **Segmentation mask** | Spatial feature map |

The conditioning context is injected at multiple resolutions in the U-Net via cross-attention, allowing fine-grained spatial control.

## Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)
---
To strengthen adherence to the conditioning signal, LDMs use **classifier-free guidance** ([Ho & Salimans, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598)):

1. Train the model with the condition randomly dropped (replaced by $\varnothing$) with probability $p_\text{uncond}$
2. At inference, interpolate between conditional and unconditional predictions:
$$
\tilde\epsilon_\theta(z_t, c) = \epsilon_\theta(z_t, \varnothing) + w \cdot (\epsilon_\theta(z_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(z_t, \varnothing))
$$
Higher guidance scale $w$ → stronger condition adherence, lower diversity; typically $w \in [7, 12]$ for text-to-image.

## Comparison: Pixel-Space vs. Latent-Space Diffusion
---

| | Pixel DDPM | LDM |
|---|---|---|
| Denoising space | $\mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ | $\mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}$ (small) |
| Training compute | High | Low ($\sim 4\text{–}48\times$ less) |
| Sample quality | High (at high NFE) | High |
| Speed | Slow | Fast (fewer ops per step) |
| Conditioning | Harder | Easy via cross-attention |
| Examples | DALL-E 1, ADM | Stable Diffusion, FLUX |

## Notable Models
---
- **Stable Diffusion 1.x / 2.x** (Stability AI): text-to-image, latent $64 \times 64 \times 4$, CLIP text encoder
- **Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL)**: larger U-Net, dual text encoders (CLIP + OpenCLIP)
- **FLUX** (Black Forest Labs): replaces U-Net with a DiT (Diffusion Transformer), rectified flow
- **DALL-E 3**: uses LDM conditioned on detailed captions generated by GPT-4
